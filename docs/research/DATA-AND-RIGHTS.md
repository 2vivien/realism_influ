# Data, Reference Images, and Rights

## Purpose

The project may study photographs, generated images, camera samples, prompt examples, and benchmark outputs. Public visibility does not automatically grant permission to redistribute an image or use it for every purpose.

## Preferred sources

1. Images created by contributors.
2. Images with explicit permission and documented releases.
3. Public-domain images with verified provenance.
4. Images under a license compatible with the intended use, with required attribution.
5. Dataset samples used under the dataset's terms and applicable restrictions.

Record the source and license for every reference asset. Do not upload full copyrighted papers, paid courses, proprietary prompt packs, or scraped Pinterest collections without permission.

## Human subjects and likeness

- Prefer consented or appropriately licensed references.
- Do not use this repository to impersonate a real person, fabricate documentary evidence, or imply that generated portraits document real events.
- Do not infer sensitive attributes from appearance.
- Take special care with minors and private individuals.
- Follow applicable law and platform disclosure rules when publishing synthetic imagery.

## Provenance labels

- **real_photo:** photographic capture with source and provenance documented.
- **ai_generated:** created by a generative model.
- **ai_edited:** a real or generated input modified by generative editing.
- **composite:** multiple sources combined.
- **unknown:** provenance not established.

Never label unknown material as a real photograph.

## Prompt rights

Link to original prompt sources. Summarize methods in original wording. Reuse exact prompts only when allowed by the license or terms.

## Scraping policy

Automated collection must respect site terms, robots directives where applicable, rate limits, authentication boundaries, and copyright restrictions. Do not bypass paywalls, logins, CAPTCHAs, or technical access controls. Prefer official APIs, open datasets, and citation metadata. Store source URLs and short notes rather than mirroring entire articles.

## Dataset manifest

For every image or subset, record asset ID, filename, provenance class, creator/source URL, license/permission, capture or generation model/settings if known, consent status where relevant, allowed uses, redistribution status, date acquired, and reviewer.
