# Photographic Realism Evaluation Rubric

Score each dimension independently. Attractiveness is not evidence of realism.

## Scale

- **1 — Clearly implausible:** major contradiction or obvious artifact.
- **2 — Weak:** noticeable issues undermine the photographic read.
- **3 — Mixed:** plausible in parts, with visible inconsistencies.
- **4 — Strong:** coherent with minor issues.
- **5 — Highly coherent:** convincing for the stated scenario at the evaluated size.
- **N/A:** not applicable.

## Dimensions

### 1. Human appearance
Facial geometry, expression, eye/teeth/ear/hand anatomy, plausible asymmetry, hairline, strands, and grooming.

### 2. Skin and materials
Texture scale matches framing/focus; pores and fine hair are integrated; highlights match the source; skin tone and local variation remain plausible; materials respond differently to light.

### 3. Lighting
Key direction matches across subject and environment; shadow direction/softness fit source geometry; catchlights and reflections have plausible sources; fill and background exposure make sense.

### 4. Camera and optics
Perspective and field of view match the intended scenario; focus and depth of field are coherent; bokeh and edge blur are plausible; distortion and flare are not arbitrary; camera claims are not treated as verified without evidence.

### 5. Color and processing
White balance fits illumination; highlight/shadow transitions are plausible; sharpening and denoising preserve detail; no excessive local contrast or synthetic sheen; noise/compression match intended capture quality.

### 6. Scene coherence
Background geometry, scale, object interactions, contact shadows, reflections, transparency, occlusion, focus, and motion are consistent.

### 7. Overall photographic plausibility
At normal viewing size, does the image read as a plausible photograph of the stated scenario? Are defects distracting? Does the capture character match the intended genre?

## Separate scores

Record photographic plausibility (1–5), technical quality (1–5), aesthetic appeal (1–5), natural human appearance (1–5), artifact severity (0–3), and confidence (low/medium/high).

## Human comparison protocol

When feasible, randomize image order and hide prompt/model metadata. Ask separately:
1. Which image is more photographically plausible?
2. Which has fewer obvious generation artifacts?
3. Which is more aesthetically pleasing?
4. How confident are you?

Record sample size and viewing conditions. For claims about real-versus-generated discrimination, use a suitable experimental design and avoid using the same images to tune and validate a detector.
